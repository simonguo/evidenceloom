# Professional analyst and independent researcher acceptance

The confirmed target users are professional analysts and independent researchers. Their primary task is to form and scrutinize an evidence-backed research report, retain its limitations, and share a version that another researcher can inspect. The product objective remains open. This protocol defines proposed acceptance work; it does not report a user study, expert approval or competitive ranking.

## Participants and inputs

Record the participant's relevant research experience, the task they normally perform, the dataset rights, the exact software revision, the saved report/source identities, the device, language and assistive technology. Use consented public or explicitly authorized research inputs. Participant identity belongs in the study's private records; published results should use an agreed pseudonymous identifier. Current external participants and expert-approved claims are both zero.

Keep two distinct input tracks. Owned fictional reports exercise interface and failure states without representing real financial research. Real source snapshots and engineering-authored cases exercise retained-source traceability; they do not become production research runs or expert judgments. A realistic professional research assessment additionally needs complete reports from representative production workflows, retained source context and independently adjudicated labels. The current BLS snapshot cannot supply historical as-of authority.

Participants should complete tasks without the implementation author guiding each click. Record the first result, misunderstandings, failures and recovery rather than counting a second coached attempt as initial success. If a comparison is intended to measure research quality, freeze the reports and inputs, mask model/provider labels where practicable without hiding provenance needed for assessment, randomize presentation order, and record disagreements before adjudication. Engineering expected values must remain separate from external labels.

## Representative tasks

| Task | Observable result | Record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trace a report statement to its saved source | The participant opens the same version's source, identifies the saved provider attribution and its authority status (observed trace, declaration or unknown), identifies the retrieval/reference window, and distinguishes resolved citation identity from factual support. Missing metadata is stated as unknown. | Correct source/version IDs, mistaken provenance assertions, completion and recovery time, assistance used |
| Review a selected numerical statement | The participant selects the exact original literal, locates the saved cell and required units/period, checks the declared calculation or rounding, and can explain the scope of the result. A field match is not treated as approval of surrounding prose. | Exact selected span and source coordinates, computed result, context errors, unsupported confidence statements |
| Compare two saved reports | The participant chooses a requested baseline and target, identifies changed and unavailable sections, attributes each rating/configuration to its own version, and preserves the intended pair when another saved version arrives. | Selected IDs before/after arrival, wrong-version incidents, missed whitespace/state changes, completion time |
| Assess incomplete or historical inputs | The participant identifies missing, conflicting and unknown evidence, explains why a current source snapshot cannot establish an old vintage, and distinguishes insufficient-input review from a supported Hold decision. | Uncertainty correctly identified, invented historical availability, inappropriate directional conclusions |
| Share and independently reopen a report | A second participant inspects the exported version and reaches the same saved sources, identities and limitations after reopening. | Export format and identity, broken or altered references, omitted limitations, successful independent verification |
| Interrupt and resume research | The participant understands the stop/recovery state, verifies whether the original evidence was restored, and avoids confusing a running task with a completed saved version. | Visible state, observed child-process termination, input identities before/after recovery, errors and recovery steps |

For the monthly BLS case, use the dedicated source-case files and offline verification workflow. Do not insert CPI observations into stock/crypto versions or invent daily `Date`/`Close` columns to exercise the application. Application-level numerical review and export tasks require fixtures that satisfy those actual contracts.

## Measures and decision rules

For each task, retain every attempted case in the denominator. Assign one mutually exclusive primary outcome: completed, failed, abandoned or unavailable. Their counts must sum to all attempts. Record assistance as a separate flag and report each outcome with and without assistance; assisted attempts remain in the denominator. Retain reasons for unavailable inputs and timeouts. Record time from the visible task start to the first submitted answer and distinguish recovery time. When there is no submitted answer, measure elapsed time to the recorded stop, including abandonment, unavailability or timeout, and retain the reason. A click trace or passing software test is not a participant's successful research judgment.

Use independently checked numeric and source identities for objective checks. Semantic support, temporal validity, inference classification and appropriate abstention require recorded expert provenance and methods. Preserve disagreements and uncertainty rather than manufacturing one approved label. Publish both per-task counts and the underlying scope of assessment; do not combine interface completion, exact field matching and expert research approval into one confidence score.

Before evaluating competitiveness, define the same tasks, frozen source inputs, output requirements, allowed assistance and execution limits for the candidate and the participants' chosen comparison workflow. Record costs and failures with successful runs. Choose practical acceptance thresholds with the participating researchers before collecting results; this document does not invent a benchmark result or declare the candidate superior.
